# Foundation acceptance gates

## G0: authority and ownership

- Maintain one current map naming every public entity's canonical owner, projection, retention, and mutation authority.
- New goals, routines, loops, graph nodes, threads, and improvement candidates must reuse existing workstream, assignment, run, event, and knowledge identities.
- An application database, graph, model memory, or search index must not become a second source of repository truth.

## G1: causal work and public contract

- Give each managed user request one root coordinate and complete parent-child links across attempts, subagents, provider turns, tools, verification, and result.
- Keep unmanaged work explicitly unmanaged and assign typed reasons to missing links.
- Publish versions and compatibility policy for public command, query, event, subscription, failure, idempotency, and capability contracts.
- The CLI and one local reference client must consume this boundary without storage imports.

## G2: resource and latency evidence

- Managed golden journeys must produce complete or typed-partial resource reports without double-counting cumulative provider usage.
- Keep primary work, subagents, Lervo overhead, verification, and deliberation separate.
- Do not confuse estimated, provider-reported, billed, and unavailable cost states.
- Critical paths must expose stage spans and representative cold, warm, scale, and concurrency baselines with p50, p95, p99, maximum, failure, CPU, and memory observations.
- Direct hooks must meet the reviewed latency budget or remain availability-safe without falsely claiming success.

## G3: failure, concurrency, and recovery

- Every state-changing operation must declare retry, cancellation, uncertainty, compensation, and recovery semantics.
- Inject failures at every durable boundary and prove exact completion, rollback, or `recovery_required`.
- Duplicate delivery and repeated recovery must be idempotent.
- Deterministically test scheduler admission, queue bounds, fairness, deadlines, lease expiry, fan-out, joins, and backpressure.

## G4: security and privacy

- Maintain an integrated threat model covering repository content, models, capabilities, external systems, products, and multi-repository or multi-tenant boundaries.
- Every telemetry field must declare purpose, privacy, retention, cardinality, and export policy; secret and high-cardinality regression tests must pass.
- Third-party extensions must use an exact, revocable, resource-bounded isolation boundary and common conformance suite.
- Local execution and recovery must remain possible without a Hub or extension.

## G5: compatibility, capacity, and drift

- Replay historical fixtures through current readers and pass mixed supported-version tests.
- Rehearse upgrade, failed upgrade, rollback, and migration idempotence.
- Keep small, medium, large, and concurrent-workforce profiles within published bounds; every growing collection must have retention or compaction.
- Run provider, model, retrieval, and adapter drift against frozen journeys before activation and through bounded post-activation observation.
